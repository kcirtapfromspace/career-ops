### Safety-Critical Systems Angle

Patrick's strongest differentiator is building data systems in environments where getting it wrong has real consequences:
- **HHS Head Start:** Data integrity for a $15.8B federal program serving 800K+ children. Dedup errors meant misallocated federal funds.
- **VA.gov Watchtower:** Observability for systems serving 18M+ veterans. Incident response for safety-critical federal infrastructure.
- **eCR Pipeline:** Real-time public health data — errors in case reporting affect disease surveillance.
- **FedRAMP/PCI:** Compliance frameworks that enforce strict data handling, audit trails, and access controls.

This maps conceptually to Anthropic's safeguards mission: both domains demand extreme care with sensitive data, compliance rigor, and systems that fail safely. The framing works, but it requires a narrative bridge — federal compliance is not the same as AI safety compliance (HIPAA, EU privacy regs, zero data retention for enterprise AI customers).

### Summary

Strong data infrastructure IC background. Genuine experience with sensitive data in regulated environments. The critical gap is **dedicated people management experience** — 4+ years is a hard requirement, and Patrick's management is more accurately described as tech lead with direct reports, not a full-time EM.

## C — Level & Strategy

### Level Assessment

Anthropic is asking for a front-line Engineering Manager with:
- 4+ years dedicated people management (not tech lead with reports)
- Ownership of team strategy, hiring, career development
- Cross-team coordination and planning

Patrick's profile is **Staff IC who led a small team.** The role at Ad Hoc was Staff Data Architect, not Engineering Manager. Leading 4 engineers while contributing 90% IC is tech lead territory, not EM territory. The distinction matters at Anthropic, where EM is a dedicated management track.

### Positioning Strategy

**Frame the transition honestly:**
1. **Lead with the "why now" for management:** "After 10+ years shipping critical infrastructure as an IC, I've reached the point where my highest leverage is building and growing the teams that build these systems. My time leading the VA.gov Watchtower team confirmed that."
2. **Emphasize the IC depth as an asset:** Anthropic's JD explicitly calls for "hands-on software engineering experience as an IC prior to moving into management." This is a strength — many EMs at this level have been pure managers for years and have lost technical edge.
3. **Address the gap directly in the application:** Don't pretend to have 4+ years of EM experience. Instead, frame the Ad Hoc experience as "managing a team of engineers delivering mission-critical infrastructure" and acknowledge the desire to go deeper into management.
4. **Leverage the safety-critical angle:** Few EM candidates have built data systems where errors affect $15.8B programs or 18M veterans. This is a genuine differentiator for a Safeguards team.

### Risk

Anthropic asks "Do you have at least 3 years of people management experience?" as a required application question. Patrick's answer is borderline — the Ad Hoc team lead role was ~3 years, but at 90% IC it's debatable whether it counts as "people management" in the way Anthropic means it. This could be a screener-level rejection risk.

## F — Interview Plan

### STAR+R Stories (10 stories)

#### 1. HHS Head Start — Data Integrity at Scale (LEAD STORY)
- **Situation:** HHS Head Start, $15.8B federal program serving 800K+ children, suffered from duplicate records caused by spelling variations in free-form submissions, corrupting reporting accuracy.
- **Task:** Build a deduplication system that could identify contextual duplicates in real-time without false positives — errors meant misallocated federal funds.
- **Action:** Designed semantic dedup using BERT sentence transformers with lazy model loading and batch vectorization to hit sub-200ms latency within Cloud Foundry's 2GB memory ceiling. Deployed production NLP pipeline (FastAPI, SpaCy, AWS Comprehend).
- **Result:** Eliminated duplicate records, improved reporting accuracy for 250K+ program staff, enabled real-time goal suggestions.
- **Reflection:** "This taught me that data integrity in safety-critical systems isn't about clever algorithms — it's about understanding the downstream consequences of every data decision. A false positive dedup meant a child lost services. That mindset maps directly to safeguards work."
- **Map to JD:** Data integrity, PII handling, privacy-aware ML pipelines, operating under strict constraints.

#### 2. VA.gov Watchtower — Building Observability for Safety-Critical Infrastructure
- **Situation:** VA.gov served 18M+ veterans with no unified way to detect cross-system incidents. Teams used Datadog, GitHub, Slack, and PagerDuty independently.
- **Task:** Build a centralized observability platform and lead a team to deliver it.
- **Action:** Architected multi-source ETL pipeline (Python, Parquet, DuckDB) with quarterly consolidation. Led team of 4 engineers. Implemented DORA metrics and Four Golden Signals as standards across 40+ product teams.
- **Result:** Platform became VA's standard for incident management and engineering performance measurement.
- **Reflection:** "Leading this team was my transition from pure IC to people management. I learned that the hardest part isn't the architecture — it's aligning 40 teams on a shared standard without having authority over any of them."
- **Map to JD:** Team leadership, cross-functional coordination, data infrastructure at scale, building data products.

#### 3. Colorado Payroll — Migration Without Downtime
- **Situation:** Colorado state payroll system ran on legacy mainframe. 100K+ employee records needed migration to AWS without disrupting pay runs.
- **Task:** Design and execute zero-downtime migration with 30+ years of schema drift to reconcile.
- **Action:** Built idempotent ELT pipelines (dbt, DuckDB, Redshift) with sub-hour refresh latency. Architected secure data enclave (Step Functions, Fargate, Terraform). Built reconciliation layer mapping legacy schema changes to modern data contracts.
- **Result:** Successful migration, 30% cloud cost reduction, sub-hour refresh (down from batch-weekly).
- **Reflection:** "Migrating 30 years of schema drift is essentially building data contracts retroactively. Every decision about how to map old to new had compliance implications — payroll data is some of the most regulated data there is."
- **Map to JD:** Data portability across environments, data contracts, compliance, migration strategy.

#### 4. Argo Workflows Platform — Kubernetes-Native Data Orchestration (Workiva)
- **Situation:** Workiva's Data Management & Analytics team needed a scalable pipeline platform. Existing approach was manual, deployment took days.
- **Task:** Architect an enterprise data pipeline platform on Kubernetes.
- **Action:** Built Argo Workflows platform with CloudFormation (Aurora PostgreSQL multi-AZ, S3 + KMS encryption, Okta SSO). Implemented Helm charts for multi-cluster deployment with Prometheus and New Relic observability.
- **Result:** Pipeline deployment time reduced from days to hours. Standardized workflow templates enabled self-service automation.
- **Reflection:** "Multi-cluster deployment taught me that portability is a first-class design concern, not a retrofit. You either build for multiple environments from day one or you pay the tax later."
- **Map to JD:** Multi-cloud/multi-region portability, data infrastructure at scale, platform engineering.

#### 5. eCR Public Health Pipeline — Real-Time Sensitive Data Processing
- **Situation:** Public health agencies needed real-time electronic case reporting (eCR) ingestion at 50K+ events/day with identity resolution and NLP enrichment.
- **Task:** Build a scalable, privacy-aware pipeline for sensitive health data.
- **Action:** Designed real-time ingestion pipeline (HL7, FHIR, AIMS) with sub-second latency. Integrated AWS Comprehend Medical for NLP and Verato for identity resolution. Built modular rules registry (Lambda, DynamoDB, S3).
- **Result:** 50K+ events/day at sub-second latency with automated data handling and reduced duplication.
- **Reflection:** "Health data is the closest I've come to HIPAA-level constraints. Every design decision had to be vetted against what data could be stored, how long, and who could access it."
- **Map to JD:** HIPAA-adjacent, privacy-preserving data pipelines, PII handling, compliance.

#### 6. Data Mesh at Workiva — Governance at Scale
- **Situation:** 200+ analysts needed access to domain-specific data products, but governance and access control were ad hoc.
- **Task:** Implement a data mesh architecture with proper RBAC without slowing analysts down.
- **Action:** Pioneered Data Mesh using AWS Lake Formation with cell-level tag-based RBAC. Built Python ETL/Reverse ETL workflows. Automated provisioning via Terraform and CloudFormation.
- **Result:** 200+ analysts could access domain-specific data products with strict governance maintained.
- **Reflection:** "Cell-level RBAC on a data lake is essentially the same problem as privacy-preserving data interfaces — how do you give people access to the data they need without exposing what they shouldn't see?"
- **Map to JD:** Privacy-safe data APIs, access constraints, data governance.

#### 7. Vail Resorts — PCI Compliance at Scale
- **Situation:** $300M eCommerce platform required PCI compliance across distributed infrastructure (IIS, MSSQL, F5, VMware).
- **Task:** Maintain compliance during large-scale server migrations and TLS remediation.
- **Action:** Remediated legacy TLS protocols, championed HTTPS migrations, managed PCI compliance through infrastructure changes. Built real-time monitoring with Splunk.
- **Result:** Maintained PCI compliance through multiple migration cycles while handling millions of concurrent users during peak seasons.
- **Reflection:** "PCI taught me that compliance isn't a checkbox — it's an operational discipline. Every infrastructure change has compliance implications, and you need processes that catch that before production."
- **Map to JD:** Compliance frameworks, regulated environments, infrastructure management.

#### 8. Amazon Fulfillment Center — Operating at Scale Under Pressure
- **Situation:** 800K sqft fulfillment center needed IT infrastructure for $5M building expansion.
- **Task:** Serve as interim IT Project Manager to launch the expansion on schedule.
- **Action:** Managed end-to-end IT buildout: 1,000+ camera system, 100 switches, 50 servers, wireless audit/remediation. Mentored junior technicians.
- **Result:** Launched on schedule. Developed root cause analysis processes that reduced recurring incidents.
- **Reflection:** "Amazon was where I learned that at scale, the system is the product. Individual heroics don't work — you need processes, monitoring, and people who know what to do when things break."
- **Map to JD:** Team mentoring, incident response, operating at scale.

#### 9. Splunk SmartStore Optimization — Cost vs. Performance
- **Situation:** Workiva's Splunk deployment was expensive and underperforming.
- **Task:** Reduce costs without degrading query performance.
- **Action:** Drove adoption of SmartStore architecture.
- **Result:** 50% cost reduction, 20% query performance improvement.
- **Reflection:** "Infrastructure optimization is always about tradeoffs. The same thinking applies to data retention — how do you maximize what you keep within the constraints you have?"
- **Map to JD:** "Drive tooling and architecture decisions that maximize data retention within the bounds of privacy and compliance requirements."

#### 10. Rust Side Projects — Staying Technical as a Manager
- **Situation:** Transitioning from IC to management creates a risk of losing technical edge.
- **Task:** Maintain hands-on coding ability while growing management skills.
- **Action:** Built multiple Rust projects: Sudoku engine (cross-platform), offleash (multi-tenant booking with advisory locking), off-quant (local LLM tooling), Modern Data Stack Demo (full CDC lakehouse).
- **Result:** Active GitHub with production-quality Rust code. Demonstrates continued technical depth.
- **Reflection:** "At Anthropic, I expect the EM role to be more management-heavy, but having the technical depth to review data pipeline architectures and challenge design decisions is non-negotiable."
- **Map to JD:** "Hands-on software engineering experience as an individual contributor prior to moving into management."

### Anthropic-Specific Framing

Anthropic values AI safety above all. Frame every answer through the lens of **"I've spent my career building systems where data errors have real-world consequences."** The safeguards team exists to prevent AI from causing harm — Patrick's career has been about preventing systems from causing harm to veterans, children, state employees, and public health. The domain is different but the mindset is the same.
